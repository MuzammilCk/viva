# VIVA Business Team — Business Model (Upgraded)

> The conceptual/strategic layer: what VIVA is, how it operates, and why customers choose it. Operational facts (phone, hours, tech stack, content status) live separately in `03-viva-business-context.md` so this document stays stable when those details change.

---

## 1. Business Identity

**Public brand:** VIVA Business Team
**Registered/trade name:** RIMS — same business, appears on official records; not used as the public-facing brand name (see `03-viva-business-context.md` §5 for exact display handling)
**Location:** Kottakkal, Malappuram district, Kerala
**Positioning:** A professional audio solutions business — not a generic electronics shop, and not a personal-founder brand, even though it is currently operated by one expert.

## 2. Core Operating Model

VIVA is not a product-selling business that happens to offer installation. It's the reverse:

> **An expert-led audio solutions business that understands customer requirements, recommends or engineers the appropriate solution, supplies or sources the required equipment, and personally executes installation, customization, repair, tuning, and support.**

Products are components of a solution, not the endpoint of the transaction. The operating equation:

```text
Customer requirement + environment + constraints + budget
        → expert analysis
        → solution design
        → product selection / sourcing
        → quotation
        → execution
        → testing
        → support
```

Every function in that chain — consultation, design, sourcing, installation, repair, tuning, after-sales — is currently performed by one person. There are no independent technicians or employees. This isn't a limitation to hide; it's the source of the consistency and craftsmanship VIVA sells. But it does mean the business model, and everything built on top of it, has to work at the pace and bandwidth of one expert, not a team. That constraint resurfaces in §19 below and again in `06-viva-refactor-blueprint.md` when deciding how content gets kept current.

## 3. What Brings Customers to VIVA

1. A specific speaker, amplifier, woofer, or component.
2. A complete sound system for a car, bus, or auto rickshaw.
3. A home theatre.
4. Audio installation for a café, restaurant, shop, or business.
5. An existing system that has stopped working.
6. Repair of old or faulty equipment.
7. A custom, non-standard requirement.
8. Inspection of a vehicle or site with a recommendation.
9. Advice on the best setup for a given room, vehicle, or business.
10. Restoration of an old system.

Customers arrive anywhere from fully decided ("I need this exact amplifier") to having no technical vocabulary at all ("I just want it to sound good"). The model has to serve both without making the non-technical customer feel unwelcome or the technical customer feel patronized.

## 4. Customer Entry Modes

### Mode A — Direct Product Purchase
Customer knows exactly what they want. VIVA supplies it, and may additionally install it.

### Mode B — Guided Recommendation
Customer knows the outcome, not the product. *"I want better bass in my car."* VIVA factors in need, budget, and environment, then recommends — sometimes by physically demonstrating a few options so the customer can hear the difference and choose.

### Mode C — Complete Solution Engineering
Customer has an environment and a goal, not a component list. *"What's the best setup for my café?"* VIVA weighs physical structure, space, environment, requirements, technical constraints, budget, and product availability, then proposes a multi-product solution including installation, configuration, tuning, and customization.

### Mode D — Repair & Diagnosis
Customer explains a problem → VIVA inspects → diagnoses → quotes → repairs → tests. Final scope is often unknown until inspection.

### Mode E — Custom Solution / Build
When nothing off-the-shelf satisfies the requirement, VIVA customizes or builds something specifically for the customer. Requirement-driven, not a predefined configuration workflow.

## 5. Service Environments

**Vehicles:** cars, buses, auto rickshaws — speakers, amplifiers, woofers/subwoofers, complete setups, installation, repair, upgrades.

**Non-vehicle:** homes, home theatres, cafés, restaurants, shops, offices, auditoriums, schools, mosques, events, outdoor spaces, and other environments as they arise.

In both cases, the solution is engineered to the specific structure, space, environment, requirement, and budget — never a fixed package applied uniformly.

---

## 6. End-to-End Customer Workflow

1. Customer contacts VIVA (visit, call, or WhatsApp).
2. Customer explains the requirement.
3. VIVA may request photos, examples, or details of previous work for context.
4. If needed, VIVA inspects the vehicle, site, or existing system.
5. VIVA recommends components and/or designs the solution.
6. VIVA prepares and sends a quotation.
7. Customer reviews; components can be negotiated or swapped (e.g., a cheaper amplifier).
8. Once agreed: products are sourced, pulled from stock, or specially ordered.
9. VIVA performs installation, service, or customization.
10. The system is tested and configured.
11. Customer pays per the agreed structure.
12. VIVA remains available for future repair, tuning, upgrades, maintenance, and follow-up.

---

## 7. Consultation & Inspection

Consultation, site visits, and inspections are **not charged separately** — they're part of understanding the requirement and proposing a solution. Some solutions can be scoped remotely via photos, examples, and calls; others genuinely require physical inspection. Which applies depends on the project, not a fixed rule.

---

## 8. Geography

VIVA serves broadly, including projects well outside Kottakkal — a Bangalore business commissioned a full setup, with VIVA traveling to execute it. The business should not be positioned as a strictly local/neighborhood provider. At the same time, avoid unsupported claims of guaranteed nationwide logistics until that's operationally formalized — describe willingness and track record, not promises the business can't yet back up.

---

## 9. Budget-Driven Solutioning

The model is not "one requirement → one universally best product." It's:

> Customer requirement + environment + budget → the appropriate solution for *that* customer.

Two customers with the same general need may walk away with different products depending on what they're willing to spend. This is a feature of the expertise being sold, not an inconsistency to paper over.

---

## 10. Inventory & Sourcing Model

Hybrid model:
- High-selling products kept physically in stock.
- Frequently recommended products kept on hand.
- Multiple brands carried — brand isn't fixed to one manufacturer.
- Stocked products used where they fit; otherwise sourced or specially ordered per job.

**Live inventory is explicitly not part of phase 1.** The website must never imply it knows real-time stock quantities. Revisit later only against real operational need — not before.

---

## 11. Product Range

Speakers, woofers, subwoofers, amplifiers, tweeters, microphones, wiring, accessories, and other relevant audio components. Selection is driven by product quality, suitability, customer needs, budget, availability, and project requirements — never locked to a single brand.

---

## 12. Product Showcase Strategy

The catalog exists to answer *"what does VIVA stock, supply, and recommend?"* — not to function as a shopping cart. Product entries carry name, brand, model, images, specifications, suitable use-cases, and a genuine, manually maintained price.

**Pricing rules (non-negotiable):**
- No fabricated prices.
- No invented discounts.
- No false urgency.
- No auto-guessed market prices.
- Where a real price isn't yet documented, the product shows "Contact for price" — never a blank or a fake figure.

---

## 13. Installation & Service Pricing

Separate from product pricing. Installation/service charges are generally smaller than component cost, vary by job, and aren't fixed globally. A full quotation may include product costs, quantities, brand/model, installation/labor, travel charge, customization/fabrication charge, warranty terms, and total. Customers can negotiate individual line items — a quotation is an **iterative proposal**, not a locked order.

---

## 14. Payment Model (Operational, Not Website-Facing)

For larger projects: VIVA designs the solution → sends a quotation → customer agrees → customer funds the product sourcing → VIVA purchases and installs → the service/installation fee is collected on completion. This is real internal workflow context for future business tooling — **online checkout stays explicitly out of scope for the website.**

## 15. Why Customers Choose VIVA Over Buying Online

Anyone can buy an amplifier or speaker online. What that purchase doesn't answer:

- Is this right for my use case, and compatible with what I already have?
- Is it suitable for my specific vehicle or room?
- How should it be installed and the environment configured?
- Which combination actually produces the result I want?
- Who installs it, tunes it, and fixes it if something's wrong later?

VIVA's value is the combination of field experience, expert recommendation, environment-specific design, installation, customization, repair, tuning, and ongoing support. The sharpest way to say it:

> **Tell VIVA what you want to achieve. VIVA determines the right solution and makes it work.**

---

## 16. Customer Segments

Individual car owners, auto rickshaw drivers/owners, bus owners/operators, café owners, homeowners, businesses, event organizers, garages, other shops, contractors, institutions, repeat customers, and occasional B2B customers. Technical literacy spans the full range — non-technical ("I want good sound, tell me what I need"), semi-informed ("I need a woofer and amp but don't know which models"), and highly technical ("I want this exact configuration"). Nothing in the model should force a non-technical visitor to parse a spec sheet to get help.

---

## 17. After-Sales

Warranty handling where the manufacturer/brand provides it, installation warranty aligned with the original provider's terms where applicable, repair support, adjustments, tuning, upgrades, follow-up visits, and maintenance. Customers are expected to return over time for improvements — a long-term relationship model, not a one-off sale.

---

## 18. Tone of Voice *(new)*

VIVA should read as a **local expert who's genuinely good at this** — not a corporate installer, not a discount electronics stall. Practically:

- Confident about capability, plain about limitations — never overclaims logistics or guarantees it can't back.
- Speaks in outcomes ("stronger bass without distortion in a small car cabin") before specs, but doesn't dumb down for the customer who wants specs.
- No exclamation-mark sales energy, no "hurry, limited stock," no marketplace hype patterns.
- Warm and direct, the way a trusted local specialist talks to a customer in person — not stiff corporate copy translated from a template.

This tone carries through every page in `04-viva-website-architecture.md` and every content pattern in `05-viva-ui-ux-direction.md`.

---

## 19. One-Person Operation: A Design Constraint, Not Just a Fact *(new)*

Because every function above runs through one person, the website's content model has to be genuinely low-friction to keep current — otherwise it goes stale within a month and starts contradicting the "professional, active business" positioning it's meant to project. This directly shapes decisions in `06-viva-refactor-blueprint.md` (how projects/products get added) and is why phase 1 deliberately avoids anything — live inventory, complex quote workflows, customer accounts — that would require ongoing operational effort beyond what one person can sustain alongside actually doing the audio work.
